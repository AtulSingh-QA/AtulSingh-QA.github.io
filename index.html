📡 Section 1

Decoding the Jargon

The AI industry buries the truth in technical language. Here's what they actually mean — in plain English, no CS degree required.

Term 01 — LLM

Large Language Model

A software system trained on hundreds of billions of words from the internet. It learns statistical patterns — which word likely follows another — at an almost incomprehensible scale.

Plain English: A very sophisticated autocomplete. It doesn't "think." It predicts. But its predictions are so good, they're replacing entire job categories.
Term 02 — Neural Network

Neural Network

A layered mathematical architecture loosely inspired by how neurons connect in a brain. Data flows through millions of "nodes" that each apply a small mathematical transformation.

Plain English: Imagine a massive factory assembly line where each worker does one tiny operation on a number. The final output is a decision — "this is a cat," or "generate this sentence."
Term 03 — Generative AI

Generative AI

AI systems capable of creating new content — text, images, audio, video, code — rather than just classifying or analyzing existing content. GPT-4, Gemini, Midjourney, and Sora are examples.

Plain English: AI that produces things. A copywriter, graphic designer, video editor, and programmer rolled into one — available 24/7 for a few dollars a month.
Term 04 — Parameters

Parameters (Weights)

The billions of numerical values inside a model that are adjusted during training. GPT-4 is estimated to have over 1 trillion parameters. Each one is a tiny dial controlling how the model processes information.

Plain English: Think of parameters as the model's "brain cells." More parameters = more nuanced understanding = more jobs it can do. GPT-3 had 175 billion. Models today have trillions.
Term 05 — Inference

Inference

The process of running a trained AI model to generate an output. When you type a prompt into ChatGPT and get a response, that's inference. Training happens once; inference happens billions of times per day.

Plain English: Inference is the "use" phase. It's also where the electricity and water cost explodes — every single query burns real energy in a real building somewhere.
Term 06 — GPU Cluster

GPU Cluster

Thousands of Graphics Processing Units networked together to run AI workloads in parallel. NVIDIA's H100 — the dominant AI chip — costs $30,000 per unit. A single training run uses tens of thousands of them.

Plain English: The engine of the AI revolution. Controlled almost entirely by one company (NVIDIA). Without GPU clusters, none of this exists — and they consume as much power as small cities.
⚡ Section 2

The Grid Monsters

Data centers are the physical body of AI. What they consume — electricity, water, land — is staggering. And it's accelerating faster than any government or grid can handle.

⚡

Electricity: A Nation-Scale Appetite

Data centers already consume roughly 2% of global electricity. By 2030, that figure is projected to hit 8–10%. A single hyperscale AI data center draws 100–500 megawatts — the same as a mid-sized city.

500 MW Power per hyperscale facility
💧

Water: The Hidden Cost Nobody Talks About

AI data centers use evaporative cooling — water evaporates to keep GPU clusters from melting. Microsoft's data centers consumed 1.7 billion gallons of water in 2022 alone. Training GPT-4 is estimated to have used 700,000 litres.

700K L Water to train one large AI model
🌍

Land: The Data Center Land Grab

A single modern data center campus requires 100–500 acres. Tech giants are buying agricultural land, draining local water tables, and building in regions already under drought stress — Virginia, Arizona, Ireland, Singapore.

500 acres Typical hyperscale campus footprint
🔥

Heat: The Waste Nobody Sees

For every watt of computing power, roughly another watt is wasted as heat. Some facilities are experimenting with heating city buildings with this waste heat — but most of it simply bleeds into the atmosphere.

50% Energy lost as waste heat
🏗

Construction: The $1 Trillion Build-Out

Microsoft, Google, Amazon, and Meta have collectively pledged over $1 trillion in data center infrastructure spending through 2030. New facilities are being built faster than electrical grids can be upgraded to supply them.

$1T+ Committed infrastructure spend by 2030
🌑

Carbon: The Green Lie

Every major tech company claims carbon neutrality through "offsets" — essentially paying others to plant trees. Meanwhile, their actual electricity consumption from fossil-fuel grids grows exponentially. The math doesn't work.

62% Global data center power still fossil-fuel sourced

By 2027, AI data centers are projected to consume more electricity than the entire country of Japan. This isn't a prediction. It's a trajectory based on current build rates. The grid was not built for this.
